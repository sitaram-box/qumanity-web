Religious Text Rights & Permissions

Update, 2026-11-08: the Poems Marquee feature described on this page has been discontinued. Gita Press denied permission for the Hindi Vinaya Patrika translation (tradition 1 below); rather than remove only that one collection, the owner chose to discontinue the entire Marquee and every public religion-based identity symbol that accompanied it. No religious text of any kind, from any tradition — including the ones whose rights were already fully cleared — is displayed anywhere on the live site today. This page is kept as the historical record of the rights research that was done for each tradition while the feature existed, and of the Gita Press denial specifically; it is not describing anything currently visible on qumanity.in.

Qumanity's poems marquee was built to show short, rotating verses from several spiritual traditions — but it only ever displayed a text once a specific edition, translation, or transcription's publication and redistribution rights had been verified, not simply because the underlying scripture is old or because a text is readable somewhere else online. Public access to a text on another website does not, by itself, grant Qumanity permission to copy or redistribute it, and an ancient original being in the public domain does not automatically make a modern translation of it free to reuse. The sections below record, honestly, what that research found for each tradition.

Qumanity does not claim to own any religious scripture, and nothing on this page should be read as a claim of ownership, endorsement, or exclusive authority over any tradition's text. Where we describe a text as "unavailable," that reflects the state of our own research and permissions — not a rights-holder's refusal, unless a specific source explicitly declined or restricted reuse, in which case we say so plainly below.

We welcome communication from publishers, translators, institutions, and other authorized rights-holders who can provide licensing information, permissions, or corrections. See the contact section at the end of this page.

1. Hindu — Gita Press Vinaya Patrika

Qumanity has been interested in making Goswami Tulsidas's Vinaya Patrika available to Hindu members, using the Hindi translation and commentary published by Gita Press, Gorakhpur. No verified publication permission for this specific translation has currently been established in our project records, and no text from it is reproduced anywhere on this website or in the Qumanity app. The Gita Press text is therefore not presented in the public Marquee. The default Hindu Marquee currently uses a separate, newly-written collection of original Hindi devotional poetry, composed for Qumanity — it is not copied, translated, adapted, or closely paraphrased from the Gita Press translation or from any other existing work, and it is never described as Gita Press or Vinaya Patrika content. A formal request for permission has already been sent to Gita Press; see our dedicated Gita Press notice for the full history. We invite Gita Press or an authorized rights-holder to provide written permission or clarification covering website display, digital redistribution, and any relevant translation or edition rights.

2. Muslim — Quran

The original Arabic Quran text (Surah Al-Fatiha, Uthmani Mushaf script, via the Tanzil Project) is available in the Marquee today, under Tanzil's own Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 licence and its stated conditions — unmodified verbatim text only, with visible credit to the Tanzil Project and a link to tanzil.net. An Urdu translation remains unavailable: Tanzil's own published terms for translated text are not fully clear to us about redistributing excerpts on a third-party website, and we have not yet confirmed the current rights status of specific named Urdu translations either. No Urdu translation will be displayed until its redistribution and publication permissions are established. We invite the Tanzil Project, or the relevant translation's rights-holder or authorized representative, to clarify the applicable terms.

3. Christian — Bible

The Marquee's existing English Psalms collection (King James Version, 1611) is in the public domain worldwide and is available today. As of 2026-11-02, a Hindi selection is also available: eight Psalm verses from Biblica's "Open Hindi Contemporary Version," distributed under a Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International licence. Because Qumanity selected and reformatted these verses rather than reproducing the work unmodified, this is a derivative use under that licence — our attribution follows the licence's own derivative-work wording rather than Biblica's trademarked name, and the resulting data file is itself released under the same CC BY-SA 4.0 licence. The original work by Biblica, Inc. is available for free at biblica.com and open.bible. We separately investigated a proposed 1914 British and Foreign Bible Society Hindi edition, which appears to be in the public domain, but it still requires a reliable, verified digital transcription before it can be shown — the digitized copy we were able to examine was not sufficiently accurate for publication, and it remains unavailable. We invite the relevant publisher or rights-holder — including Biblica, for this or any other edition — to provide a clean authorized digital edition or written licensing clarification.

4. Sikh — Gurbani

Qumanity is researching the original Gurmukhi text of the Guru Granth Sahib and suitable translations, but no Gurbani text is currently shown anywhere on the site. The rights status of the digital editions and translations we have examined has not yet been established sufficiently for redistribution — some of the repositories we investigated state restrictive terms on their own text datasets, even where their software is separately open-source. No Gurbani collection will be published without verified source provenance and appropriate permission. We do not assume that any single organization necessarily owns all Gurbani rights, and we invite Khalis Foundation, BaniDB representatives, publishers, translators, or other relevant rights-holders to provide clarification or permission.

5. Buddhist — Dhammapada

The Marquee's Pali and English Dhammapada collections (Chapter 1, verses 1–5 — Pali via SuttaCentral, English translated by F. Max Müller, 1881, via Project Gutenberg) are available today under their documented public-domain sourcing. A Hindi translation has not yet been approved for publication — we are seeking a reliable Hindi edition with verified rights and attribution requirements, and have not yet confirmed that any specific candidate edition is safe to reuse. We invite the relevant Hindi translator, publisher, or rights-holder to provide an authorized digital edition or licensing details.

6. Jain — Jain scriptures and texts

The Marquee's underlying architecture can support Jain texts the same way it supports every other tradition, but no specific Jain text, edition, or translation has yet been approved for publication. We are researching suitable primary texts, including the Tattvartha Sutra — a text with wide acceptance across Jain traditions — and other appropriate Jain works, such as the Namokar Mantra. The rights of modern translations and digital editions of these texts must be checked separately from the rights of the ancient originals, and we have not yet confirmed a source that is clearly safe to reuse. Jain tradition includes real diversity across its communities (including the Digambara and Svetambara traditions, among others), and we do not intend for any one text or translation we eventually select to represent every Jain tradition. We invite Jain institutions, publishers, translators, and other relevant rights-holders to suggest suitable texts and clarify reuse permissions.

Where things stand today

The Marquee itself is discontinued (see the update at the top of this page) — no text from any tradition is displayed on the site today, regardless of its rights status. Before that decision, rights research had cleared the original Qumanity Hindu Hindi poetry, the English and Hindi Psalms (King James Version and Biblica Open Hindi Contemporary Version), the Arabic Quran (Surah Al-Fatiha), and the Dhammapada in Pali and English for display, while the Gita Press Vinaya Patrika translation, the Urdu Quran, the 1914 Hindi Bible edition, a Hindi Dhammapada, and any Jain text remained pending. This page is left in place as that historical record, not as a description of anything currently live.
