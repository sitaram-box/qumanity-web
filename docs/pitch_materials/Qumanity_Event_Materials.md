## Slide 2 — The Problem
**Headline:** Roughly 640,000 villages. No shared, verifiable record of what happens in them.
- A citizen raises an issue and cannot see where it went, who received it, or what happened next.
- Identity, records and participation are scattered across separate portals that do not connect.
- Civic effort (settling a dispute, cleaning a pond) earns no lasting, visible recognition.
- Trust fails because nothing leaves a trail.

## Slide 3 — The Solution
**Headline:** One identity. One public record. Eight nested levels, from Village to Earth.
- Every member gets one permanent ID (a 9-digit Private ID and a PIN) — no email required.
- Every village, tehsil, district, state and country has its own public board.
- Posts rise level by level through member votes, and the path they took stays visible.
- Reputation (Karma) comes from civic action. It cannot be bought and is never convertible to Qoins.

# 4. Q&A PREP SHEET

### 1. "If you are open-source and a DPG, how will you make money?"
"Three layers, in order. Today it's grants and CSR funding, which is how most public infrastructure starts. In year two or three, governments and panchayats that adopt Qumanity can pay for deployment and ongoing support. From year three, we offer training and certification for elected councils. The code stays free. Members are never charged, we don't sell data, and we don't sell Qoins. The money comes from institutions that want it to work well, not from the people using it."

### 2. "How do you get villagers who don't have smartphones to use this?"
"Today it needs a phone or a browser, and I won't pretend otherwise. This round funds the fix: a shared village kiosk prototype, so one terminal serves a whole community. We also start in villages that already have connectivity and prove the model there. Two new pilot villages with local coordinators are part of this round."

### 3. "Why do you use the word 'Quantum'?"
"It's a philosophy, not a physics claim. Qumanity means Quantum plus Humanity. In quantum systems, everything is connected, and nothing happens in isolation. Our structure works the same way: every voice links back to where it started. The technology itself is deliberately ordinary, so it can run anywhere and be maintained by anyone."

### 4. "How is this different from WhatsApp or existing government portals?"
"WhatsApp is private chat. It leaves no public record and no accountability. Government portals are mostly one-way: you submit, and it disappears. Qumanity is a public, nested record — one identity, one board per community, and a visible path upward with member votes deciding what rises. Standing comes from civic contribution, not follower counts."

### 5. "You are a solo founder. How will you scale?"
"The first step of this round is to stop being solo. ₹8 lakhs funds a part-time engineer, and ₹2 lakhs sets up the legal entity and IP properly so others can join safely. The code is open, so the community can contribute. The nested design scales by adding places, not by redesigning. I've done 15 years of research and built the foundation: identity, a 635,693-village map, boards and a pilot. What I need now are partners and a small team."

### Bonus questions to expect

**"Only 27 members?"**
"Yes — real people, no padding. We have deliberately not chased numbers before the core is solid. The goal of this round isn't a bigger number; it's three villages with real councils and a way to include people without smartphones."

**"Is the election pilot real?"**
"It's a pilot in one Delhi village, Rohini Sector 24, and it tests the full cycle: nomination, voting and results. [Be ready to say plainly that the voting so far used simulated participants, not 500 real residents.] Real-resident elections are what the two new pilot villages are for."

**"What do you do with a member's data?"**
"We don't sell it. A member's identity is a Private ID and a PIN with no email required, and a member's exact GPS coordinates are never shown to anyone else."

**"What are the legal and IP arrangements?"**
"That's what ₹2 lakhs is for: forming an LLP, assigning the copyright from me to the entity, and registering the trademark. The code stays open-source; the name and the entity are protected."
